Muse Can Now Buy Your Black Friday Deals at Walmart: Agent Profiles, Spending Limits, and Checkout in the Chat

The Verge on November 23, 2026 reports that Walmart's Agent Gateway is live with Muse. You set up an Agent Profile in your Walmart account, set a spending limit, approve purchases, and check out in the conversation on a card saved at Walmart, at Walmart.com prices, with Walmart returns. A Walmart post from October 21, 2026 explains the controls. No Meta post and no user purchase report were found on November 23.

By Marvin Jones

Last checked against the live product: November 23, 2026.

Start here if you are shopping this week: set a low spending limit first, and leave approval on for every purchase. The Verge on November 23, 2026 reports that Walmart's Agent Gateway is live with Muse, with checkout in the conversation on a card saved at Walmart, at Walmart.com prices, and with Walmart returns. A Walmart post from October 21, 2026 explains the controls. No Meta post and no user purchase report were found on November 23, and the launch date moved twice that day, so check your own account before you count on access.

How the flow works, in Walmart's steps as The Verge printed them

The Verge printed three steps from Walmart, and I am keeping them in that order because the order is the control.

First, you tell Muse what you need. The examples in the steps are a TV deal and Black Friday deals available for delivery. Muse helps you explore products and build a basket.

Second, you set your shopping rules. Muse takes you to Walmart to sign in, then brings you back to Muse. The first time you add an item to your cart, you set up your Agent Profile on Walmart, inside your Walmart account, including a spending limit you are comfortable with.

Third, you check out. You complete the purchase inside the conversation, using a card saved in your Walmart account, at the same prices you would find on Walmart.com. If something is not right, Walmart's return policy applies.

No dollar amount for a limit, no approval threshold, and no specific Black Friday price appears in The Verge article. Any site that prints a Black Friday price for Muse at Walmart without naming the product page it came from is printing a number this record does not have.

What an Agent Profile is, on Walmart's October 21 post

An Agent Profile is a dedicated space for agent activity inside your Walmart customer account, per The Verge. Walmart's October 21 post adds the parts a reader needs before money moves.

The profile is created automatically the first time an agent shops through the Gateway, and you authorize it by signing in to Walmart. You can delete the profile at any time. Agent purchases show separately in the profile, so you can check the purchase history for everything bought by Muse without mixing it into your own orders.

Approval is the default. Walmart's post says customers must approve agent purchases by default, and Walmart may require additional approvals even for purchases inside an agent's spending limit. You have two settings to choose between. You can require approval for every purchase. Or you can give the agent permission to make purchases inside a spending limit you set, with $50, $100, or $200 given as examples in the post.

That second setting is not a blank check, on Walmart's own wording. The default is approval, the limit is yours to set, and Walmart reserves extra approvals inside the limit. Set the limit before the first purchase, not after a basket is built, because the profile setup step in The Verge's steps happens at the first cart addition.

Field note

Payment stays at Walmart. Agents do not receive your payment card information and cannot add or change your payment methods, per Walmart's October 21 post. Checkout uses a card saved in your Walmart account, per The Verge. Those two statements fit together. Muse can complete a purchase on the saved card. It does not get the card number, and it cannot swap in a different card. If the saved card is wrong, you fix it in your Walmart account, not in the chat.

Prices and returns, on the record

Walmart's October 21 post puts the price rule in one sentence, in its own words: we price products, not people, so the prices an AI agent sees are the same prices a human would see on Walmart.com. The Verge's checkout step says the same thing in shorter form: same Walmart prices as Walmart.com.

Returns follow the same pattern. Walmart's return process applies, including if an agent does not act as intended, per the October 21 post. The Verge's step says Walmart's return policy still applies. I am printing the stronger October wording because a reader who gets a wrong item from an agent purchase will need it. The return right does not depend on the agent having followed your instructions correctly.

One limit on the history side deserves its own line. The Gateway helps an agent find relevant products for you without sharing your purchase history, per The Verge's summary of Walmart's position. Walmart's October post says the Gateway can help an agent find a customer's usual items without sharing the customer's broader shopping history. Usual items in, broader history not shared. That is the boundary as both sources state it, and this post does not stretch it into a full privacy policy, because neither source prints one here.

Caution

Do not treat a Black Friday week launch as a settled checkout. The launch date moved twice in one day on November 23, the Walmart.com Gateway page still calls the program an early beta with invitation only access, and no user purchase report was found on launch day. If you try this, start with a low limit, require approval for every purchase, and check the Agent Profile history after the first order. A limit you set at $50 for a first test tells you more than a limit you set at $200 because a deal was about to expire.

Walmart opened its gateway. Amazon and eBay blocked agents.

The contrast is in The Verge's reporting, and it is worth printing without adding to it. Amazon and eBay have blocked AI agents from browsing their platforms, per The Verge. Walmart says support for Muse is just the beginning, and additional Walmart shopping experiences for other AI agents and apps are on the way, per The Verge.

That is a business choice by three different retailers, stated in one article. It is not a general rule about agent shopping in November 2026. Expedia hotels in Muse, live since November 9 on this site's record, is a booking partner flow, not a retailer gateway. Loblaw in Canada, filed on this site on November 18, is a grocery partner. Walmart is the first live retailer Agent Gateway purchase flow for Muse in this site's record, and that first is the reason this post exists during Black Friday week, not in a roundup later.

Quick answers

Is Muse live at Walmart now?

Yes, on The Verge's November 23, 2026 report, read in full that day. Walmart's Agent Gateway is live with Muse support, with other apps and agents on the way. Walmart told The Verge the launch would be delayed to November 24, then told The Verge it was no longer delayed and would roll out on November 23, so access may vary by account during rollout.

What is a Walmart Agent Profile?

A dedicated space for agent activity inside your Walmart customer account, per The Verge. Walmart's October 21, 2026 post says the profile is created automatically the first time an agent shops through the Gateway, you authorize it by signing in to Walmart, you can delete it at any time, and agent purchases show separately in it.

How do I set a spending limit?

Set it when you set up the Agent Profile, at the first cart addition, per the steps The Verge printed from Walmart. Walmart's October 21 post says you can require approval for every purchase, or allow purchases inside a limit you set, with $50, $100, or $200 as examples. Customers must approve purchases by default, and Walmart may require extra approvals even inside a limit.

What card gets charged?

A card saved in your Walmart account, per The Verge. Agents do not receive your card information and cannot add or change your payment methods, per Walmart's October 21 post.

Are the prices the same as Walmart.com?

Yes, on both sources. The Verge's checkout step says same Walmart prices as Walmart.com, and Walmart's October 21 post says the prices an agent sees are the same prices a human would see on Walmart.com.

What happens if Muse buys the wrong thing?

Walmart's return process applies, including if an agent does not act as intended, per Walmart's October 21 post. The Verge's step says Walmart's return policy still applies.

Has anyone reported an actual purchase yet?

No report was found on November 23, 2026. Searches that day found launch coverage and October explainers only, and no first person account of a completed purchase, a limit working, or an approval prompt working. This site rechecks for the December 2, 2026 follow up.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Do I need a separate Walmart account for Muse?

A: No, on the sources read here. The Agent Profile sits inside your Walmart customer account, per The Verge, and you authorize it by signing in to Walmart, per Walmart's October 21, 2026 post.

Q: Can Muse shop at Walmart without my approval?

A: Only inside a limit you set, and even then not always. Walmart's October 21 post says customers must approve agent purchases by default, you can require approval for every purchase, and Walmart may require additional approvals even for purchases inside an agent's spending limit.

Q: Does Walmart share my whole purchase history with Muse?

A: No, on the boundary both sources state. The Gateway helps an agent find relevant products without sharing your purchase history, per The Verge, and Walmart's October post says it can help find your usual items without sharing your broader shopping history.

Q: Is this available to everyone right away?

A: Not settled on November 23. The Verge reports the Gateway live with Muse, while the Walmart.com Gateway page read the same day still says early beta, access limited, available by invitation only, with no mention of Muse in its readable text. Check your own Walmart account and your Muse chat for the sign in step before assuming access.

Q: Can Muse use a card that is not saved at Walmart?

A: No route like that appears in the sources read here. Checkout uses a card saved in your Walmart account, per The Verge, and agents cannot add or change payment methods, per Walmart's October 21 post.

Q: How is this different from Amazon or eBay?

A: Amazon and eBay have blocked AI agents from browsing their platforms, per The Verge on November 23, 2026. Walmart has opened an Agent Gateway with profiles, limits, and approval, and says more agent support is on the way, per the same report.
