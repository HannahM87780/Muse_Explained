Is Muse Available in My Country?

US, Canada, and Mexico per Meta's own rollout materials — but the guide sites are still arguing about it. Here's the honest map, what's frozen, and why the VPN tutorials are skating on thin ice.

By Marvin Jones

Last checked against the live product: September 27, 2026.

This is one of the most-asked Muse questions online, and the answer keeps shifting underfoot. Meta's own materials say three countries. A stack of guides updated this week say one or two. Nobody has a timeline for anyone else. Here's what each side is saying, and which one to trust.

The short answer

The United States, Canada, and Mexico. That's the list in Meta's Connect 2026 materials (published September 24), which added Mexico to the US and Canada. GSMArena's September 24 write-up repeats the three-country list.

The catch: as of this morning, some of the most thorough independent guides still don't list Mexico. One major Muse guide (checked September 27) says Muse is "still US-only" and that "Meta announced no new countries at Connect." Others list only the US and Canada. The guides are split, and Meta's support pages haven't settled it with a clean official country list of their own.

Field note

When Meta's own event materials and the independent guides disagree, trust Meta's materials for what Meta says — and treat the guides' caution as a reminder that Mexico's rollout may be partial, staggered, or simply badly communicated. If you're in Mexico, the honest answer is "probably yes, try it" — not "definitely."

The rollout so far

  • September 8, 2026 — launch day, United States only: iOS, Android, muse.ai on the web, and inside WhatsApp as a contact.
  • September 18 — Canada joins. Meta announced it with an X post ("Made it to Canada. Where to next? 👀"), confirmed by the Canadian press the same day.
  • September 24 — Meta's Connect materials list Mexico alongside the US and Canada. This is the source of the Mexico confirmation — it came from Meta's event, not a press report.

Ten days from one country to (maybe) three. Fast by normal rollout standards, and it still left out most of the planet.

What's frozen: the UK, the EU, and India

Meta has published no timeline for any other country — not the UK, not the EU, not India, not anyone else. No waitlist, no "coming soon" page, no hints on earnings calls. Just silence.

India is the interesting holdout. The Indian tech press reported at launch that India was excluded with no date, and nothing has changed since. The regulatory backdrop is worth knowing: an Indian parliamentary committee has a live proposal requiring human oversight of agentic payments — exactly the kind of rule a product that spends money on your behalf would have to answer. And India has 500M+ WhatsApp users, which is presumably why everyone assumes Muse lands there eventually. Assume away; Meta hasn't confirmed a thing.

One more detail: Meta's two biggest distribution channels are a separate lever from the international rollout. On September 27, chief AI officer Alexandr Wang announced you can now add Muse to your Instagram profile — the first floodgate opening. The WhatsApp signup door, though, is still closed even in the countries that have Muse.

Why the rollout is moving at this speed

Meta hasn't explained the pace, but the context is public: usage has run roughly 10x Meta's own projections, the service has shown "partially degraded" status with search failures as the top complaint, and every user gets a dedicated virtual machine — which makes this a much harder scaling problem than a chatbot. Expanding to billions of potential users before the infrastructure is steady would be a good way to break the thing. The slow rollout is the cautious one.

The VPN question

This is where the internet gets creative. There are now multiple published tutorials for signing up from outside the supported countries:

  1. The VPN method — a popular referral thread's pinned tutorial: connect a VPN to a US city, open muse.ai in an incognito window, register with an email address, and (their advice) don't use an email tied to Facebook or Instagram while signing up from outside the US. A YouTube tutorial with a similar recipe adds a US address, a phone number, and switching your App Store region.
  2. The cloud-computer method — the same thread's evolved version: use Google's Gemini Spark, a US-based cloud computer, to open muse.ai and register from there. The claim is that registering from a US cloud machine may skip the age-verification check entirely.

Here's what makes this a thin-ice situation rather than a neat trick:

  • The block is account-level, not IP-based. muse.ai loads from anywhere in the world — it's the sign-in that's restricted to accounts in supported countries. A VPN changes what the site sees; it doesn't necessarily change what Meta's account system believes about you.
  • Meta has said nothing about accounts created this way. No terms language, no warning, no blessing. If Meta later tightens the country check, there's no promise a VPN-created account keeps working.
  • The age gate complicates the trick. Muse is 18+, and reviewers have been asked to verify with a credit card. A foreign-currency card — or the cloud-computer route's "skip the check" claim — is exactly the kind of thing a fraud system notices.
  • The referral thread isn't a neutral source. It exists to harvest invite-code redemptions (it publishes a code to paste). Its instructions may work, but its incentives are its own.
Caution

Signing up via VPN or a cloud computer is a workaround, not a feature. If you do it, treat the account as potentially fragile: don't connect real bank accounts or hand it credentials you can't afford to lose access to. The safest play is waiting for Meta to launch in your country — unglamorous, but free of account risk.

How to check your own country

The only authoritative source is Meta itself: muse.ai's signup flow and Meta's official Muse pages. If sign-in works for your account, you're in. Third-party guides — including this one — are snapshots that go stale fast; Meta updates its materials without warning, and as the Mexico disagreement shows, the guide layer doesn't always keep up.

What to watch

The next country added, whenever it happens, will likely arrive the way Canada did: a quiet X post, not a press release. I'll update this page when the list grows, when Meta publishes an official country list, or when there's any movement on the UK, EU, or India. The WhatsApp signup door is a separate watch item — when that opens, the rollout math changes completely. (Instagram's door opened on September 27.)

Quick answers

Is Muse available in my country?

If you're in the US, Canada, or Mexico — Meta's three listed countries — yes. Everywhere else, no official availability and no published timeline.

Why do some guides say Muse is US-only?

Meta added countries through event materials rather than a single official country list, and guides haven't all caught up. Meta's September 24 Connect materials list the US, Canada, and Mexico; some guides checked this week still say US-only.

Can I use a VPN to sign up from another country?

Tutorials exist, but Meta hasn't said whether accounts created that way keep working — and the block is account-level, not IP-based, so a VPN doesn't guarantee a durable account. Treat it as a workaround with unknown risk.

When is Muse coming to the UK / EU / India?

Meta hasn't given any timeline. India was explicitly excluded at launch and nothing has changed since.

Why is the rollout so slow?

Meta hasn't said, but usage has run far above its projections and each user gets a dedicated VM — scaling is genuinely hard here. The still-closed WhatsApp signup channel also suggests Meta is deliberately throttling demand.

I'm traveling to the US. Can I sign up while I'm there?

Unknown. Since the restriction is on the account's country, not your IP address, a US trip may not be enough — and Meta hasn't addressed travelers at all. Don't plan a trip around it.

Does the Mexico listing mean full availability there?

Not necessarily. Mexico appears in Meta's Connect materials, but the rollout may be partial or staggered, and some guides still don't list it. If you're in Mexico, try signing up — the flow itself is the test.

Will Meta punish VPN-created accounts?

Nobody knows — Meta hasn't addressed it. The risk is that a later country-check tightens and the account stops working or gets flagged. The cautious move is to wait for an official launch.

Is there a waitlist I can join?

No public waitlist exists. Since September 25 there's an early-access program you join by asking Muse directly — but it's aimed at Connect-announced features, not country expansion.
