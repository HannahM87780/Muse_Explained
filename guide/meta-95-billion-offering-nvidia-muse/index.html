Meta seeks up to $95 billion, and Nvidia is putting $3 billion behind Muse

The headline number is a ceiling, not a check that has cleared. About $35 billion in stock is launched now, up to $60 billion more could follow from early 2027, and the part with Muse's name on it is Nvidia's $3 billion stake and a wider deal to cut the cost of running Muse.

By Marvin Jones

Last checked against the live product: October 27, 2026.

Verdict up front: this is the biggest Muse story of the week, and it is a finance story before it is a product story. No new feature was announced. No date was promised for anything a user can tap. What changed is the scale of the money behind the assistant, and who is supplying part of it.

What was actually announced

CNBC and Bloomberg reported on October 26, 2026 that Meta is raising up to $95 billion in a stock offering, in common and preferred stock, to fund AI infrastructure and superintelligence work. Reuters and The Wall Street Journal reported on October 27 that Nvidia is investing $3 billion in that offering, and that Nvidia and Meta are deepening their partnership on the Muse AI assistant.

The $95 billion figure needs its structure attached, or it misleads. Reporting carried by Morningstar and Dow Jones, and by The Guardian on October 26, breaks it into two parts: about $35 billion in common and preferred stock launched now, plus an at-the-market program of up to $60 billion that could begin in the first quarter of 2027. Add the two parts and you get the ceiling. Meta has not raised $95 billion. It has set out a path that could reach that number.

CNBC reported on October 27 that AMD is also investing $1 billion. That puts confirmed chip supplier money at $4 billion, $3 billion from Nvidia and $1 billion from AMD.

What I am not printing as settled

Two slots in this story are still open, and this page will not fill them with weaker sourcing.

No major outlet has confirmed a final priced total for the offering. Lower figures have circulated from secondary sources, and they conflict with each other. Until a major outlet or Meta itself confirms a priced total, the honest numbers are the ones above: up to $95 billion sought, about $35 billion launched now, up to $60 billion in a later program.

Broadcom's position is the second open slot. CNBC's October 26 coverage named Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom as eyeing up to $5 billion in shares combined. No source checked on October 27 gave Broadcom a solo amount, and one truncated snippet suggesting a much larger per-company figure conflicts with that combined framing. Broadcom's amount stays unconfirmed here.

Why Muse is named in a stock offering at all

NVIDIA published its own account in its newsroom on October 26. The expanded partnership with Meta covers Muse and the Muse family of models, and its stated aim is improving inference and training performance.

Those two words carry the user-facing weight of this deal, so they are worth unpacking plainly. Training is building and improving the models. Inference is running them, once per question, per task, per agent run. A personal assistant that works in the background, checks inboxes and works through multi-step jobs burns inference every time it acts. An assistant priced at free, $20 a month and $100 a month has to be run at a cost those prices can support, or the losses grow with every new user.

That is the connection between a stock offering and the app on your phone. It is also where I stop, because the next step is guesswork. NVIDIA's statement gives an aim, not a result. It does not promise faster answers on a specific date, a higher token ceiling, or a price change, and neither Meta nor Nvidia announced any of those things this week. If the partnership works, the signs would show up in the product first: steadier response times at busy hours, weekly token ceilings that hold or rise, paid tiers that stay at $20 and $100. None of that has been shown yet.

What this does not change today

Your plan is the same price it was last week. Sweeps of the pricing guides on October 27 held at free, $20 for Power and $100 for Maximum, with no change dated to this news. Your token ceiling has not been announced as changing. The Japan rollout, the connector list and the missing Windows app are all exactly where they were on Monday.

That gap between the money headline and the unchanged product is the story's uncomfortable half, and it belongs in the post. Meta is asking public markets for a sum larger than the annual budgets of most countries' research programs, in the name of infrastructure and superintelligence, while the assistant that money partly supports still has no native Windows app and a pricing page its own users argue about in comment threads. Both halves are true at once. Readers deciding whether to build their routines around Muse should hold both.

Quick answers

How much is Meta raising?

Up to $95 billion, structured as about $35 billion in common and preferred stock launched now plus an at-the-market program of up to $60 billion that could begin in the first quarter of 2027. That is a ceiling and a plan, not a confirmed final total.

How much is Nvidia putting in?

$3 billion, per Reuters and The Wall Street Journal on October 27, 2026. AMD is putting in $1 billion, per CNBC, for $4 billion in confirmed chip supplier money.

What does Nvidia get, and what does Muse get?

Per NVIDIA's newsroom statement on October 26, the two companies are expanding their partnership on Muse and the Muse family of models, aimed at improving inference and training performance. No feature, date or price change was announced with it.

Has Broadcom invested, and how much?

Unconfirmed. October 26 coverage named Broadcom alongside Nvidia and AMD under a combined figure of up to $5 billion in shares. No solo Broadcom amount was confirmed in the sources checked on October 27.

Will Muse get cheaper or faster because of this?

Nobody has promised either. The partnership's stated aim is better inference and training performance. Whether that reaches users as speed, higher token ceilings or steadier prices is an open question this site will track in the funding ledger above.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is the $95 billion for Muse alone?

A: No. CNBC and Bloomberg describe the offering as funding AI infrastructure and superintelligence work across Meta. Muse is named in the Nvidia partnership that sits inside the deal, which is why an assistant blog is covering a stock offering at all.

Q: Where did the $95 billion figure come from?

A: CNBC and Bloomberg reported the up-to-$95 billion offering on October 26, 2026. The split into about $35 billion launched now and up to $60 billion in a later at-the-market program comes from Morningstar and Dow Jones reporting and The Guardian, both on October 26.

Q: I saw a lower total for this offering elsewhere. Which number is right?

A: Lower priced totals are circulating from secondary sources and they conflict with each other. No major outlet checked on October 27 had confirmed a final priced total, so this page uses the sought and launched figures from major outlets and leaves the priced total open.

Q: Does this affect the Muse subscription I already pay for?

A: Nothing announced this week changes a price, a tier or a token ceiling. Pricing guides checked on October 27 still listed free, $20 for Power and $100 for Maximum.

Q: What happens next with this story?

A: Three things can fill the open slots: a confirmed priced total, a confirmed Broadcom amount, and the first user-visible result of the Nvidia partnership. This page's funding ledger will be updated when any of them lands, starting with tomorrow's check of the offering coverage.
