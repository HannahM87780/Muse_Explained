Getting Started: Setup + Your First Goal

The full first hour: install, sign up, connect one service, and hand Muse a real job — with the mistakes worth skipping.

Last checked against the live product: September 18, 2026.

Most people set up Muse the way they set up any app — tap through, connect everything, ask it something vague — and then wonder why it feels underwhelming. The first hour matters more than it looks. Here's the version that actually works.

Step 1: Install (10 minutes)

Pick your surface: the iOS or Android app, muse.ai on the web, or WhatsApp (message Muse like a contact). US only, 18 and older, as of this writing.

Sign-up asks for a payment card even on the free tier — it's there so rising usage doesn't interrupt your work, not a charge. You'll also meet the usage meter, which shows your remaining allowance as a percentage. Glance at it now so you recognize it later.

Don't skip

Have an invite code? Redeem it now: Settings → Redeem invite code, within 48 hours of joining. Both sides have been reported to receive a token grant (1 billion Muse tokens each, per third-party trackers and Meta's promo — promotional, subject to change). Miss the window and it's gone.

Step 2: Make it yours (5 minutes)

Before any real work, do the small personalization pass: give Muse a name, pick an avatar, and set the proactivity level. This isn't vanity — naming it changes how you brief it (you write to someone, not something), and proactivity controls whether it surfaces suggestions unprompted or stays quiet until asked. Start conservative; you can turn it up later.

Step 3: Connect exactly one service (10 minutes)

Ask Muse ("connect my Gmail") or go to Settings → Connectors, tap Connect, read what's shown, and authorize. Just one. Email is the best first connector for most people — it's where the highest-leverage early tasks live.

Read the access description before you tap through. This is also the moment to internalize the rule: a connector is a door you opened. You'll add more doors later, deliberately.

Step 4: Run your first delegation (20 minutes)

Pick one job you already understand — today's unread email is the canonical choice. Write the brief in three parts:

  1. Outcome: "Go through today's unread email. Give me three buckets: needs my reply today, can wait, junk. For each item in bucket one, one line on why."
  2. Boundaries: "Don't send, delete, or archive anything. Don't open attachments from unknown senders."
  3. Check-in: "Show me the summary and stop. I'll tell you what to do next."

Read the result like an editor, not a fan. Where was it wrong? The most valuable sentence you'll write this week is the correction: "treat anything from someone I've emailed before as personal, regardless of formatting" fixed my triage permanently.

Field note

My first triage run surfaced two genuinely time-sensitive emails I'd have missed in the Monday pile — and misfiled one client email as a newsletter. One correction sentence fixed the pattern. The lesson wasn't "it's 85% accurate"; it was "the 15% is fixable, and the fix compounds."

Step 5: Set your first goal (10 minutes)

Now the part chatbots can't do. Give Muse something ongoing: "Every weekday at 8am, summarize my unread email from the last 24 hours the same way we just did." Check the Goals tab to see it tracked, and Upcoming for the schedule.

Then — and this is the discipline — watch the first two runs before you trust it. A recurring task with a bad brief fails on a schedule. Two reviewed runs are the price of admission for automation.

Step 6: Learn the approval ladder (ongoing)

The first time Muse asks to do something sensitive, you'll see the approval options: allow once, for the task, for the site, always for the connector, or deny. Default to allow once. Widen only for patterns you've watched work. Peek at the Activity log (tap your assistant avatar) after the first few tasks — it's the fastest way to understand what the agent actually does when you're not watching.

Mistakes to skip

  • Connecting everything on day one. Each connector is surface area. Earn them one at a time.
  • Vague briefs. "Help with email" gets you a lecture about email. Outcomes, boundaries, check-ins.
  • Automating before reviewing. Two clean runs before anything recurring. No exceptions.
  • Reflex approvals. Read the prompt — especially amounts, recipients, and unfamiliar sites.
  • Pre-paying. Run free until the usage meter tells you otherwise. The upgrade takes seconds.

Do the hour above and you'll be past the stage where most people stall — an installed app they ask trivia. You'll have a working delegation, a goal running, and a feel for the approval rhythm. That's the whole game; everything else is repetition at larger scale.

Quick answers

How do I get started with Muse?

Install the app (or use muse.ai / WhatsApp), sign up with a card on file, redeem any invite code within 48 hours, connect one service, and delegate one familiar job. The steps above are the full first hour.

What should my first task be?

Something you understand well enough to grade — email triage is the classic. Familiarity lets you calibrate the tool.

How do I redeem an invite code?

Settings → Redeem invite code, within 48 hours of joining. Your own code is behind the Invite button, top right of the main chat screen.

How do I make Muse do something regularly?

Give it a goal in plain language ("every weekday at 8am, do X"). Track it in the Goals tab, review the first two runs, then let it run.
