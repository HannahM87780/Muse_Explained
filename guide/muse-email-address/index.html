Your Muse Gets Its Own @muse.ai Email Address: How to Set It Up, What You Can Forward to It, and the Approve-Before-Send Control

Meta's Help Center page titled About Muse email addresses confirms that your Muse can have its own @muse.ai address, separate from your personal email, set up in the Muse app. You forward selected emails to it, Muse asks for approval before sending and you can allow or deny, and an external email you own can be verified. The page gives no country list, no free or paid rule, and no sending limit number.

By Marvin Jones

Last checked against the live product: November 21, 2026.

How setup works, on the Help Center page

The page gives one setup sentence, and this post does not stretch it. Ask your Muse to set up an email address, then follow the instructions in the Muse app.

No menu path beyond that sentence appears on the page. The snippet-only social posts mention a mail tab under your avatar. That route may be right, and a reader may well find it there, but no full read Meta page in this record states it, so this post does not print it as the setup route. If the in-app instructions differ from the social post wording, the in-app instructions are the ones to follow.

One naming point is confirmed. You can change your Muse's sender name in its email settings, and that name is what recipients see. The Help Center page does not say you can pick the part before @muse.ai, does not say handles are first come, first served, and does not say an address cannot be changed after creation. The note.com sighting reports all three points from one user's attempt in Japan. One sighting is worth printing as a sighting. It is not yet a general rule sourced to Meta.

The model is forwarding, not inbox access

This distinction runs through the whole page, and it is the reason the feature is narrower than a first headline suggests.

Your Muse's @muse.ai address is separate from your personal email account. Setting it up does not give Muse access to your Gmail, Outlook, or other inbox. The page points to Connectors as the separate route for connecting an inbox like that. A reader who wants Muse to triage a whole inbox is asking for a different product surface than the one this page describes.

What this page describes is selection by the user. You can forward selected emails to your Muse to share information, or ask it to help with tasks by email. The page's own examples are ordinary delegated work: contact a restaurant about a reservation, coordinate plans, or follow up on a refund. Its sending example is just as plain: email this restaurant and ask whether they have a table for four on Friday at 7 p.m.

That selection step is a control, not a chore to work around. You choose which emails go to Muse. An email you do not forward does not arrive through this address.

Field note

The verified external address works the same way, and the page is careful about the boundary. You can add an email address you own and verify it with Muse. Adding it lets Muse recognize instructions sent from that address as coming from you. It does not give Muse access to your inbox. After verification, you can forward emails or send instructions from that address to the @muse.ai address without switching to the Muse app, for example forwarding a newsletter with an event invitation and telling Muse to RSVP and add it to your calendar.

The warning beside it deserves its own line. Only add an account that you own and that only you can access, because anyone with access to that account can send your Muse instructions. You can manage linked external addresses in Muse Settings, and the page tells you to unlink an address at once if you lose access or your provider says the account may not be secure.

Approve before send, in the page's own steps

Sending has numbered steps on the Help Center page, and the approval step sits in the middle of them, not in a footnote.

When Muse asks for approval, review the email details. You can allow or deny the action. If something does not look right, deny the action and tell your Muse in Chat what it should change. A second section on the page, Guidance and permissions, repeats the point in general form: Muse may ask for approval before taking an action, and you should review permission requests carefully and only allow actions you are comfortable with.

You can also review sent and received emails in Muse and ask about the progress of a task. That review habit is the practical half of the control. Approval before a send stops a bad draft. The sent and received view after a send is how you check what actually left, addressed to whom, in a week when several tasks are running at once.

Caution

Email carries a stricter warning than chat on this page. Muse treats email with extra care. It may ask you to confirm requests and refuse to act on requests about sensitive topics over email, including financial information, health information, and credentials. A refusal on one of those topics over email is the page working as written, not a bug to route around by rewording the same request. Move that task into Chat, or handle it yourself.

A second check applies to mail you receive. Emails genuinely sent with Muse use an @muse.ai address. Check the full email address, especially when the sender name looks familiar, because the sender name can be changed. If you receive an email from an @muse.ai address, someone has asked their Muse to contact you or added their Muse to a conversation. You can reply from your usual email account and you do not need a Muse account.

Limits, bulk use, and misuse

Sending limits apply to emails from the @muse.ai address. If you reach a limit, Muse may be unable to send additional emails. No number, no per day figure, and no per month figure appears on the page. Any site that prints a number for this limit without naming a Meta source is printing a number this record does not have.

The intended use line is short. The address is intended for individual communication and delegated tasks, such as coordinating plans or following up on an order. It is not intended for bulk email campaigns. Meta may limit or disable Muse email features when it detects spam, scams, harassment, or other misuse.

Those two lines together set the shape of the feature. It is a personal agent's mailbox for tasks you assign, with a ceiling Meta has not published and a bulk ban Meta has published.

Deleting one email, and losing the whole address

Two different deletion records sit on the page, and mixing them up would mislead a reader who is cleaning up.

You can delete individual emails from your Muse's inbox. After you delete an email, Muse may still remember information it learned from that email. You can ask Muse to forget a topic or information it has learned. Deleting an email from Muse does not remove copies held by other recipients.

The address level record is final in a different way. If you delete your Muse's email address, reset Muse, or delete your account, you lose access to the @muse.ai address. Emails sent and received through that address will be deleted, and new emails sent to it will be undeliverable. A reader who has given the address to a school, a landlord, or a regular contact should tell those contacts before deleting or resetting, because mail sent afterward bounces with no forwarding rule stated on the page.

One data point from the page belongs here too. When you email an @muse.ai address, Meta collects and processes your message and any attachments to provide the service and carry out tasks. Information from those emails may become part of the user's interactions with Muse and may be used to develop and improve AI models, depending on the user's settings and the Muse Privacy Policy, as the page describes it. Forwarding a thread therefore sends more than the task line at the top. It sends the thread.

Quick answers

What is a Muse @muse.ai email address?

An email address for your Muse, separate from your personal email account, confirmed on Meta's Help Center page titled About Muse email addresses, read in full on November 20 and 21, 2026. Setting it up does not give Muse access to your Gmail, Outlook, or other inbox.

How do I set it up?

Ask your Muse to set up an email address, then follow the instructions in the Muse app, per the Help Center page. No longer menu path is stated on that page.

Can Muse read my whole inbox through this address?

No, on the page read here. You forward selected emails to the address, and you choose which emails to send or forward. Connecting Gmail or Outlook is a separate Connectors route, not this address.

Does Muse ask before it sends?

Yes. When Muse asks for approval, review the email details and allow or deny the action. If something does not look right, deny it and tell Muse in Chat what to change, per the Help Center page.

Can I email Muse from my own address?

Yes, after verification. You can add an email address you own and verify it, then forward emails or send instructions from that address to the @muse.ai address without switching to the Muse app. Verifying does not give Muse access to your inbox.

Is the address free, and where is it available?

Not stated on the Help Center page. The page contains no free or paid rule and no country list. AI Understanding on November 20, 2026 called it free, and one note.com sighting the same day reports a free plan creation in Japan, but no Meta primary in this record confirms a general eligibility or country rule. This site rechecks on November 28, 2026.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What happens if I delete the address, reset Muse, or delete my account?

A: You lose access to the @muse.ai address, emails sent and received through it will be deleted, and new emails sent to it will be undeliverable, per the Help Center page. Tell regular contacts first, because no forwarding rule is stated.

Q: Are there sending limits?

A: Yes, and no number is published on the page. Sending limits apply, and if you reach a limit Muse may be unable to send additional emails. That is the whole statement in the source.

Q: Can I use the address for a bulk campaign or a mailing list?

A: No, on the intended use stated on the page. The address is for individual communication and delegated tasks, not bulk email campaigns, and Meta may limit or disable email features when it detects spam, scams, harassment, or other misuse.

Q: Can I change the name recipients see?

A: Yes. You can change your Muse's sender name in its email settings, per the Help Center page. The page does not state a rule for picking or changing the part before @muse.ai. One user sighting from Japan on November 20, 2026 reports picking that part, finding a first choice taken, and being unable to change it after creation.

Q: If I delete one email, is it gone everywhere?

A: No. Deleting an email from Muse does not remove copies held by other recipients, and Muse may still remember information it learned from that email. You can ask Muse to forget a topic or information it has learned, per the page.

Q: Will Muse act on any instruction sent by email?

A: Not on the page's own warning. Muse treats email with extra care, may ask you to confirm requests, and may refuse requests about sensitive topics over email, including financial information, health information, and credentials. Only verify an external address that you own and only you can access, because anyone with access to that account can send your Muse instructions.
