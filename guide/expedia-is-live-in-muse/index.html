Expedia Is Live in Muse: What a Hotel Booking Actually Looks Like Now

Muse can now book Expedia hotels in the US. Skift reported the go-live on November 9, 2026, about six weeks after the integration was announced, and Expedia confirmed the scope to Skift directly: natural language search and booking are in, cancellations and rebooking are out. No Meta or Expedia newsroom post confirming the go-live was found on November 9.

By Marvin Jones

Last checked against the live product: November 9, 2026.

Promise Ledger: Partner Liveness, Expedia

Promise Ledger

A standing box for the Expedia question, placed here so the verdict sits next to the headline. Last updated November 9, 2026, from Skift's November 9 report, read firsthand that day, and the November 9 checks listed in Sources.

  • Announced: September 24, 2026. PhocusWire reported that US travelers would be able to describe a destination, dates, and hotel preferences to Muse, see matching Expedia options, and make a booking, with Expedia as merchant of record and payment completed through Muse. No date was given.
  • Live for hotels: November 9, 2026, per Skift. Skift reports Muse can now book Expedia hotels, and it published a screenshot, credited to Skift, of the integration in use.
  • What the screenshot and report show: Muse accessing a member profile, member prices, and hotel bookings. That wording is this site's reading of the Skift report and its screenshot, filed in the November 9 news watch.
  • Expedia confirmation: given to Skift directly, not in a newsroom post. Expedia confirmed to Skift that the integration covers natural language search and booking, but not cancellations or rebooking, and a spokesperson is quoted in the article.
  • Primary confirmation in print: not yet found. Checks on November 9 found no Meta post and no Expedia Group newsroom post announcing the go-live. The Expedia words above reached print through Skift.
  • Second outlet with its own test: not yet found. Event Planner News republished the Skift article on November 9. A republication is not a second test.
  • Flights: not shown. No source checked on November 9 shows Expedia flights, packages, or vacation rentals booking live through this integration. Hotels are the whole live record so far.
  • Next recheck, a promise with a date: this site will recheck for a Meta or Expedia primary post, a second outlet with its own test, and any flights confirmation on November 17, 2026, and will publish the result on that date even if all three are still missing.

The short version: hotels, in the US, search and book only

Yes, with limits stated in the same report that carries the news. Skift, by Adriana Lee, published November 9, 2026 at 6:00 AM EST, reports that Meta's Muse agent can now book Expedia hotels, about six weeks after the companies announced the integration. Six weeks back from November 9 lands on the September 24 announcement coverage, and the timing checks out against PhocusWire's September 24 report.

The limits are not fine print added later. They are in Skift's second and third paragraphs. Expedia Group chief executive Ariane Gorin, discussing third quarter results the previous Wednesday, said the connection was going live in the US, in her words, "potentially while we're on this call today." PYMNTS reported that earnings call on November 4, and at that point going live was still an expectation. Skift's November 9 report is the first one checked here that states it as done, with a screenshot.

Then Expedia itself narrowed the claim. In a follow-up, Expedia confirmed to Skift what the integration can and cannot do: it covers natural language search and booking, but not cancellations or rebooking.

What a booking looks like, on the record Skift published

An Expedia spokesperson, quoted by Skift, gives the working description: "Travelers can describe their destination, dates, and hotel preferences in their own words, and Muse can surface relevant hotel options from Expedia."

That is the flow a reader can count on today, stated at the weight the source gives it. You describe the trip in plain words. Muse surfaces Expedia hotel options. Booking runs through that connection, with Expedia as merchant of record, a term from the September announcement coverage that Skift's report does not change.

The member piece is the part readers will feel first. Skift's screenshot shows Muse accessing a member profile and member prices, per this site's November 9 news watch reading of the report. If that holds in ordinary use, a Muse search is not limited to the public rates a logged out visitor sees. It can work with the prices attached to your Expedia account. No source checked on November 9 states how the account is linked, what permission screen appears, or whether a member has to sign in again inside Muse. Those steps are not described in the Skift text read here, and this post does not fill them in.

What the record does not show matters as much. Skift says it ran a limited test. A limited test by one trade outlet is a real check, and it is also a small one. It does not establish that every US account sees the integration today, that every Expedia hotel is bookable through it, or that a booking completes end to end at member prices without a handoff to Expedia. Gorin's November 4 wording, reported by PYMNTS, left both paths open: Muse can complete a booking itself or send the traveler to Expedia to finish it. Which path a given booking takes is exactly what the November 17 recheck and reader reports need to settle.

Field note

The useful test this week is narrow, and it takes five minutes. Run the same hotel search twice, once in Muse and once signed in to Expedia directly, with the same dates and the same room. Write down whether the price Muse shows matches the member price on Expedia, and whether the booking finishes inside Muse or hands you over. A screenshot of a price match is worth more than a week of launch coverage, because member pricing was the claim in the screenshot and nobody outside Skift has published a check of it yet.

What you cannot do in Muse yet: cancel or rebook

Expedia's confirmation to Skift draws this line in plain terms: search and booking are in, cancellations and rebooking are out.

The spokesperson quote explains where changes go instead. "Because Expedia is the merchant of record, changes can be made through Expedia's customer support, just like when a booking is made directly with us."

Read those two statements together and the shape is clear. Muse is a place to find and book the hotel. Expedia support remains the place to change it. A traveler who books through Muse and then needs a different date should not expect the chat that made the booking to undo it. That is not a defect hidden in the terms. Expedia stated it to Skift on the record, before most readers will make their first booking. It does mean the convenience ends at the booking confirmation, and anyone whose plans move often should weigh that before choosing this route over booking direct.

Caution

Do not read "Expedia hotels are live in Muse" as "Expedia is live in Muse." The live record on November 9 covers hotels, in the US, for search and booking. It does not cover flights. Muse has handled flights through a separate connection since launch, covered elsewhere on this site, and that separate history is not evidence that Expedia flight inventory is now bookable through this integration. Packages, vacation rentals, and other Expedia inventory are in the same position: not shown, not confirmed, and not to be listed as live until a Meta or Expedia primary source, or a second outlet with its own test, states it.

Why this one took six weeks, and why the date kept slipping

The announcement in September gave no date. PhocusWire, on September 24, noted that no specific date had been given for when Expedia in Muse would be available, and this site carried the question as an open item through November 8, idea 145 on its list: named as a partner is one state, a connector page existing is a second state, and bookings actually running is a third, different state.

The November 4 earnings call was the first timing signal after September, and even it was a tease. Gorin said the connection was expected to go live in the US during the call itself. A thing that goes live during an earnings call has no launch post, no help page announcement timed to it, and no clean date a reader can check the next morning. That is how a major partner can be both live and hard to prove live for five days, until a trade reporter runs the test and publishes a screenshot.

There is a second reason to keep the sourcing straight. Before this integration, Muse's hotel searches worked in a different way: the agent browsed consumer travel sites, including Expedia and Hotels.com, the way a person would, per Skift's September testing as reported in later coverage. A browsed result is not a partner integration. Readers who saw Expedia hotels in Muse in October were not necessarily seeing this connection, and their screenshots do not date the go-live. The November 9 change is the native connection, working from Expedia prices and content, which is the arrangement All Things Muse described on November 5 when it reported the earnings call.

Who gets it, and what it costs

The go-live Gorin described, and Skift reported, is in the US. No source checked on November 9 states that the Expedia integration is live for Muse accounts in Canada, Japan, Singapore, or Mexico, the other four countries where Muse is available on this site's dated record, with Mexico added on November 5.

On price, nothing in the Skift report names a Muse plan as required for Expedia booking, and nothing changes Muse pricing. US pricing on Meta's help pages, unchanged in this site's November checks, is free with a usage limit, Power at $20 a month, and Maximum at $100 a month. Whether an Expedia booking made through Muse counts against a free account's usage limit in any particular way is not stated in any source checked on November 9. The hotel itself is paid for as an Expedia booking, with Expedia as merchant of record, per the announcement coverage and the spokesperson statement above.

One money question from the September coverage stays open. Trade press reporting at announcement said payment would be completed inside Muse. Skift's November 9 text read here does not walk through a payment screen step by step, so the exact payment step, card used, and confirmation format stay undescribed until a primary source or a fuller test report covers them.

Next: does it show your member price?

One reader question, and it is the one this post cannot yet answer from its own check: in your own Muse, does an Expedia hotel search show your member prices, and does the booking complete end to end inside Muse? Skift's screenshot says member access is part of the integration. Your account, this week, is the wider test. Answers with the hotel, the dates searched, and whether you were handed to Expedia to finish will shape the November 17 follow up more than any announcement will.

Also on November 9, so returning readers have the full day

Two other Muse stories broke on November 9. Neither displaces the Expedia check above, and both are tracked here rather than chased tonight. Meta released Muse Ember, its smallest model, on Muse Code and the Meta Model API at $0.10 per million input tokens. No consumer app availability was stated in that announcement, so Ember is a developer story until Meta says otherwise. Separately, The Times and Retail Gazette reported that Muse will launch in the UK within weeks. No date, no named UK retailer, and no GBP pricing were reported, and Meta declined to comment. Both stories stay on this site's watch list with their limits labeled, the same treatment Expedia got until a test and a screenshot settled it.

Quick answers

Is Expedia live in Muse?

Yes, for hotels, per Skift on November 9, 2026. Skift reports Muse can now book Expedia hotels in the US, about six weeks after the integration was announced, and Expedia confirmed natural language search and booking to Skift directly.

What can Muse book on Expedia?

Hotels, on the November 9 record. Expedia confirmed to Skift that the integration covers natural language search and booking. Travelers describe a destination, dates, and hotel preferences in their own words, and Muse surfaces relevant hotel options from Expedia, per the Expedia spokesperson quoted by Skift.

Can Muse book Expedia flights?

Not shown. No source checked on November 9, 2026 shows Expedia flights, packages, or vacation rentals booking live through this integration. Muse's flight booking through a separate connection is a different arrangement and does not confirm Expedia flight inventory.

Can I cancel or change a booking in Muse?

Cancellations and rebooking are not covered, per Expedia's confirmation to Skift. Because Expedia is merchant of record, changes can be made through Expedia customer support, in the spokesperson's words, just like when a booking is made directly with Expedia.

Does Muse show Expedia member prices?

Skift's screenshot shows Muse accessing a member profile, member prices, and hotel bookings, per this site's reading of the November 9 report. How the account is linked and what sign in step appears are not described in the sources checked on November 9.

Has Meta or Expedia announced the go-live in its own post?

No such post was found on November 9, 2026. Checks that day found no Meta post and no Expedia Group newsroom post announcing the go-live. Expedia's confirmation was given to Skift and quoted in its article. Event Planner News republished the Skift article, which is not an independent confirmation.

Does Expedia booking in Muse cost extra or need a paid Muse plan?

No plan requirement is named in the Skift report. Muse US pricing is unchanged: free with a usage limit, Power at $20 a month, and Maximum at $100 a month. The hotel is an Expedia booking, with Expedia as merchant of record.
