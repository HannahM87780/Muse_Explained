Muse vs Dots: OpenAI's Answer to the Always-On Agent

OpenAI launched Dots at DevDay: nameable, always-on agents on GPT-6 Astra. Here’s how they stack up against Muse on price, apps, and the stuff that actually matters.

By Marvin Jones

Last checked against the live product: September 29, 2026.

Three weeks after Muse launched, OpenAI answered. At its DevDay keynote today, Sam Altman unveiled Dots: “remarkably capable, always on agents built to handle really anything you can think of.” Barron’s wrote the comparison for me: “Like Muse AI from Meta Platforms, users can name their Dots.”

So now it’s a two-player race. Here’s the honest scorecard, with everything sourced and the unknowns labeled.

Where Dots wins on paper

Distribution, and it’s not close. Altman claimed 1.2 billion weekly ChatGPT users on stage. Even if that number is doing marketing work, Dots launches inside the biggest AI distribution channel on earth. Muse’s 2.8 million downloads in two weeks is a great start that looks tiny next to a billion.

The enterprise framing is sharper too. Specialist Dots with their own identities and credentials, Microsoft Agent 365 integration in progress, ChatGPT Space as a shared team workspace. OpenAI is selling Dots as coworkers. Muse is still pitched as your personal assistant.

And the plumbing sounds serious: 4,000+ app connections on day one, each Dot with its own cloud computer and browser. The rumored $500/month “Pro Max” tier never materialized. The actual gate is the existing $200 Pro plan.

Where Muse wins right now

Price of entry. This is the whole ballgame for normal people. Muse has a free tier and paid plans starting at $20 a month. Dots is locked behind the $200-a-month Pro plan or a $125 Business Premium seat. That’s a 10x gap just to try the thing.

Maturity: three weeks of it. Muse has been poked, reviewed, torn down, and embarrassed in public already (the iMessage incident, the Marketplace mess, reviewers counting its permission prompts). It also has the receipts: JPMorgan’s assistant benchmark scored it 9.3 overall with the fastest responses of the field (via wccftech). Dots is hours old, and its first public demo froze mid-keynote while the presenter filled dead air with “I guess Dot’s having a slow morning.”

Field note

The demo stall matters more than it looks. Both products sell the same promise: walk away and trust it to keep working. Muse earned its scars in users’ hands. Dots earned its first one on stage.

The honest unknowns

Nobody outside OpenAI has used a Dot for real work yet. Reliability, permission fatigue, the “what did it do while I wasn’t looking” questions, all unanswered. Texting support isn’t shipped. Team pricing beyond the per-seat numbers isn’t detailed. And the safety story has a wrinkle worth watching: a day before the keynote, reports said OpenAI shelved the newer GPT-6.1 Astra after safety tests showed agents acting unexpectedly. Dots runs on the older GPT-6 Astra. That’s reported, not confirmed.

Caution

Don’t buy the $200 plan for Dots on day one unless you have a specific job for it. New agents are at their buggiest at launch (Muse’s first three weeks proved that), and the free tier of the incumbent costs nothing to compare against.

What to watch

I’ll update this page when there’s movement on any of these: real-world Dots reliability reports, texting support shipping, team and Enterprise pricing details, and whether the GPT-6.1 Astra safety story gets confirmed or denied.

Quick answers

Should I switch from Muse to Dots?

Not today. Dots costs 10x more to try and is hours old. If you're already on ChatGPT Pro for other reasons, it's worth a look, otherwise wait for the reliability reports.

Is Dots a “Muse killer”?

Investors don't think so: Meta's stock rose after the announcement. The market read Dots as enterprise-first, not a consumer attack on Muse. Salesforce and ServiceNow fell instead, on the “AI eats software” read.

Which one is better at the actual work?

Unknowable today. Muse has a JPMorgan benchmark win and three weeks of independent reviews. Dots has a keynote demo, one of which froze. Check back in a month.

Can I use Dots for free?

No. Day-one access requires ChatGPT Pro ($200/month) or Business Premium ($125/month per seat), per Bloomberg's reporting. No free tier announced.

Do Dots really keep working while I'm away?

That's the pitch: persistent agents with their own cloud computer that keep working after you close the chat. The keynote demo stalling is the honest caveat: always-on is a claim, not yet a track record.

I already named my Muse. Can I name my Dot?

Yes, both are nameable. OpenAI's Holly Li: “By giving Dots their own identities, they become an extension of our team.”
